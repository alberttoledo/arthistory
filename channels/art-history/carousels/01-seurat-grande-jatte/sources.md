# Sources — 01 Seurat, A Sunday on La Grande Jatte — 1884

## Facts
- Title, dates (1884–86, border added 1888–89), oil on canvas, 207.5 × 308.1 cm, Helen Birch Bartlett Memorial Collection; many painted and drawn sketches plus larger canvases led up to it; Seurat reworked it two years after starting, adding the monkey and other figures; Greek-frieze ambition. Art Institute of Chicago API record: https://api.artic.edu/api/v1/artworks/27992 (page: https://www.artic.edu/artworks/27992/a-sunday-on-la-grande-jatte-1884)
- Painted in two campaigns, May 1884–March 1885 and October 1885–May 1886; shown at the eighth and last Impressionist exhibition, May 1886; dotted border added 1888–89; zinc yellow darkened to brown, already visible in Seurat's lifetime; acquired 1924; Sondheim/Lapine musical *Sunday in the Park with George* (1984); class mix (Everdell, *The First Moderns*). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Sunday_Afternoon_on_the_Island_of_La_Grande_Jatte
- Zinc yellow (zinc potassium chromate) darkened soon after completion: AIC conservation pages, e.g. https://archive.artic.edu/sciarttech/2e3.html
- 1886 show at 1 rue Laffitte opened May 15 without Monet, Renoir, Sisley or Caillebotte: https://www.britannica.com/art/the-eight-Impressionist-exhibitions — Neo-Impressionist works hung apart, *La Grande Jatte* dominated the room: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Camille-Pissarro/The-Impressionist-years
- Félix Fénéon coined "Neo-Impressionism" in 1886: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-impressionism
- Seurat born 2 Dec 1859, died 29 Mar 1891 (aged 31); 24 when he began in May 1884: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Seurat
- Singesse slang and pêcher/pécher pun (hedged as "many historians think"): our reel script channels/art-history/episodes/001-seurat-monkey/script.md; the Wikipedia article above notes the fishing/prostitution allusion reading.
