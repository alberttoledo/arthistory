# Sources — 02 Nighthawks (Edward Hopper, 1942)

## Facts
| Claim (slide) | Source |
|---|---|
| Oil on canvas, 84.1 × 152.4 cm, 1942, Art Institute of Chicago, Friends of American Art Collection (2) | AIC API record 111628: https://api.artic.edu/api/v1/artworks/111628 ; page https://www.artic.edu/artworks/111628/nighthawks |
| "There is no entrance to the establishment"; inspired by a restaurant on Greenwich Avenue, not a realistic transcription (4, 5) | AIC catalogue description (same record, CC-BY text) |
| Completed January 21, 1942 (3) | Wikipedia, citing the Hoppers' record book: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nighthawks_(Hopper) |
| Pearl Harbor attack Dec 7, 1941; painting finished about six weeks later (3) | Standard date; link to the war mood is commentary, kept as framing ("as the lights went down"), e.g. https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/63967/15-things-you-might-not-know-about-nighthawks |
| Sold May 13, 1942, for $3,000 to the Art Institute (3) | Wikipedia, citing Jo Hopper's record book (Lyons 1997, p. 63); also https://lithub.com/the-story-behind-edward-hoppers-iconic-painting-nighthawks/ |
| Helnwein's *Boulevard of Broken Dreams* (1984) recasts it with Bogart, Monroe, Dean, Elvis (3) | Wikipedia (Parodies section) |
| Quote: "suggested by a restaurant on Greenwich Avenue where two streets meet" / "I simplified the scene a great deal and made the restaurant bigger" (4) | Hopper, in Katharine Kuh, *The Artist's Voice* (1962), p. 134; cited by Wikipedia |
| "Sleuths still argue which corner" (4) | Mulry Square theory vs. records showing a gas station there: Jeremiah Moss, Vanishing New York https://vanishingnewyork.blogspot.com/2010/06/finding-nighthawks-part-3.html ; Gail Levin (via Wikipedia) |
| Quote: "Man night hawk (beak) in dark suit, steel grey hat, black band, blue shirt (clean) holding cigarette" (7) | Jo Hopper's record-book notes, in Deborah Lyons, *Edward Hopper: A Journal of His Work* (1997), p. 63; cited by Wikipedia. Title "Night Hawks" from the journal entry. |
| Jo's note: "Sign across top of restaurant, dark—Phillies 5¢ cigar" (8) | Lyons 1997, p. 63, via Wikipedia. The "Only 5¢" lettering is visible on the canvas. |
| Quote: "E. posed for the two men in a mirror and I for the girl." (9) | Jo Hopper, letter to Marion Hopper, Jan 22, 1942, quoted in Gail Levin, *Edward Hopper: An Intimate Biography* (2007), p. 349; cited by Wikipedia |

Hedges: the copy doesn't name which figures are "the two men" (we label the fedora man "Edward" on slide 9 as the clearest match). The door copy says the only visible door is "at the back, by the urns", with no claim about where it leads.
