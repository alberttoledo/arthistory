# Sources: No. 03, The Night Watch

## Facts
- Title, date, medium, 1715 move to the Town Hall on the Dam, cut "to fit the painting between two doors"; "Night Watch" first used in a document in 1797; attacks in 1911, 12 cuts in 1975, acid in 1990 ("only the varnish was damaged"); WWII evacuation in Sept 1939 and a cave in the St. Pietersberg, Maastricht, from 1942; the captain's hand shadow on the lieutenant's coat with Amsterdam's coat of arms ("the city is safe in the hands of the militia"). Rijksmuseum, "10 things about Rembrandt's Night Watch": https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/stories/10-things/story/10-things-about-rembrandt-s-night-watch
- Strips cut from all four sides (left 64.4 cm, right 7 cm, top 23.3 cm, bottom 11.3 cm); three lost figures on the left ("two militiamen and a young child"); Lundens copy (attr., c. 1642–55, on loan from the National Gallery, London); 2021 AI reconstruction. Rijksmuseum press release, 23 June 2021: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/press/press-releases/for-the-first-time-in-300-years-the-night-watch-is-complete-again
- The remaining painting's size of 379.5 × 453.5 cm: Rijksmuseum collection record SK-C-5, https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/SK-C-5. Wikipedia gives 363 × 437 cm.
- Dark varnish led to the nickname and was removed in the 1940s; the 1975 attack (Wilhelmus de Rijk, a bread knife); 1990 acid; Operation Night Watch from July 2019: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Watch
- 1975 attack: about a dozen slashes to Banninck Cocq and Van Ruytenburch (The Art Newspaper, 2019): https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/02/19/trimmed-splashed-and-slashed-the-anatomy-of-rembrandts-the-night-watch
- Day vs night is hedged. Rijksmuseum director Wim Pijbes in 2011 said "It's not certain that it's a night scene": https://globalnews.ca/news/170202/new-lighting-shows-off-rembrandts-famed-night-watch-as-a-vibrantly-lit-scene
- The girl: a chicken with large claws and a pistol called a klover; she "serves as the company's 'mascot'". The figure "with just one eye visible and wearing a beret may well be Rembrandt himself". Rijksmuseum story on Google Arts & Culture: https://artsandculture.google.com/story/dALSivMco4xIJA
- Quote: "I would have preferred him to put more light into it." Samuel van Hoogstraten, *Inleyding tot de hooge schoole der schilderkonst* (1678), in English translation. He also said the other pieces in the hall stand beside it "like playing cards". Translation as given in the Columbia Art Humanities reader: https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/arthumanities/websites/remmon/pdf/art_hum_reading_31.pdf . See also Britannica: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Rembrandt-van-Rijn/Night-Watch
