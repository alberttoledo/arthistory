# Sources: 05 The Calling of Saint Matthew (Caravaggio, 1599–1600)

## Facts
- Basic data (oil on canvas, 322 × 340 cm, 1599–1600, Contarelli Chapel, San Luigi dei Francesi). The Matthew debate: majority view = the bearded man (Irving Lavin, "Me?"). Angela Hass argues for the young man counting coins. Christ's hand ≈ mirror of Adam's hand in Michelangelo's *Creation of Adam*. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Calling_of_Saint_Matthew
- Contract signed 23 July 1599. Installed by July 1600. "Caravaggio's first major public commission… cemented his reputation." Side paintings lit "by a beam coming from the only source of natural light." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contarelli_Chapel
- History of the debate: Andreas Prater (1985, *Pantheon*), Hass and Kretschmer (1988), rejected by Röttgen (1991) and Lavin (1993), revived by Sara Magister (2012). Radiographs show Christ's arm "in un primo stadio… un po' più rialzata", and the standing apostle (Peter) added later. Avvenire, 1 Aug 2012: https://www.avvenire.it/agora/pagine/caravaggio-chi-e-il-vero-matteo
- Diagnostics show Caravaggio "aggiunse la figura di Pietro a opera conclusa", possibly to stress papal authority (hedged as "some historians think"). Artribune: https://www.artribune.com/?p=718424
- The light comes from the right, matching the chapel's real window. The Calling hangs left of the altar, perpendicular to the only natural light source. ASC: https://theasc.com/?p=52433 ; Faith Magazine: https://faithmag.com/calling-saint-matthew
- Biography: born 29 Sep 1571. Contarelli paintings "an immediate sensation". Killed Ranuccio Tomassoni on 29 May 1606, sentenced to death, fled Rome. Died 18 July 1610 at Porto Ercole, aged 38. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaggio
- (Caption option, not used on slides.) Pope Francis on the painting: "This is me, a sinner on whom the Lord has turned his gaze." From the 2013 interview with Antonio Spadaro, "A Big Heart Open to God", America Magazine. Cited on the Wikipedia painting page.
