# The street that drowned in molasses

On this Boston street, people once drowned in molasses.

January fifteenth, 1919. An unusually warm day in the North End. Just after noon, a giant tank holding over two million gallons of molasses burst.

A brown wave, up to twenty-five feet high, rolled down Commercial Street at around thirty-five miles an hour.

It bent the steel of the elevated railway, flattened buildings, and swept up horses, wagons and people.

Twenty-one people died. About a hundred and fifty were hurt. Rescuers waded through syrup up to their waists.

The tank had been built too thin and never properly tested. It leaked so badly, it was painted brown to hide the stains.

The court case that followed helped change how America builds things like it. And some locals swear that on hot days, you can still smell it.
