# Caesar salad isn't Roman

The Caesar salad has nothing to do with Julius Caesar.

It was invented in Tijuana, Mexico, in the 1920s, by an Italian immigrant chef named Caesar Cardini.

During Prohibition, Americans crossed the border to eat and drink freely. Caesar's restaurant was packed.

The story goes that on the Fourth of July, 1924, the kitchen was running low. So Cardini made a salad from what he had, and tossed it at the table, for show.

The original had no chicken, and no anchovies. Just romaine, garlic, olive oil, lemon, Worcestershire sauce, a coddled egg, Parmesan, and croutons.

And you ate the whole leaves with your fingers.

So next time you order one, thank Tijuana. Follow for the stories behind your food.
