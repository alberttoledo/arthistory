# The ship that sailed with no one aboard

In 1872, a ship was found sailing across the Atlantic with no one on board.

Her name was the Mary Celeste. She had left New York a month earlier with seventeen hundred barrels of alcohol, seven crew, and Captain Benjamin Briggs, with his wife and two-year-old daughter.

On December fourth, sailors on another ship, the Dei Gratia, spotted her drifting east of the Azores. They climbed aboard.

The cargo was almost untouched. There was six months of food and water. The crew's belongings were still there.

But the lifeboat was gone. So were the captain's navigation instruments, and the ship's papers. The last log entry was nine days old.

Nine of the barrels were empty. One theory says the captain feared an explosion, and ordered everyone into the lifeboat to wait it out. Then the line snapped.

No trace of the ten people on board was ever found.
