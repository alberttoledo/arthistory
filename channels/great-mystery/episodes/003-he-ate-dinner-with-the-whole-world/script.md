# He Ate Dinner With the Whole World
# Generated from: Three people who never solved the mystery of life and dove into it anyway: Leonardo da Vinci (notebooks, curiosity about everything), Anthony Bourdain (eating with strangers, travelling to meet people), Richard Feynman (the pleasure of finding things out, bongos, safecracking).

Anthony Bourdain never solved life's mystery. So he pulled up a plastic stool.

He spent decades in restaurant kitchens. Then, in his forties, a book about that life made him famous.

So he left the kitchen. For years he travelled with a camera crew. He didn't go for the monuments. He went to eat with whoever would have him.

Street stalls in Vietnam. Family kitchens. Places he couldn't pronounce, with people who didn't speak his language.

In Hanoi, in 2016, he shared noodles with a sitting president. Bún chả, cold beer, plastic stools. The bill came to about six dollars.

He never claimed to have answers. What he had was a habit. Sit down. Eat what's offered.

A meal is the fastest way into someone else's life. It's hard to stay a stranger for long once you've shared a table.

So tonight, eat somewhere you can't pronounce. Ask what's good. Then stay a while.
