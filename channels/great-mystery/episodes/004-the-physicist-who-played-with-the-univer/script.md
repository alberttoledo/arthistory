# The physicist who played with the universe
# Generated from: Three people who never solved the mystery of life and dove into it anyway: Leonardo da Vinci (notebooks, curiosity about everything), Anthony Bourdain (eating with strangers, travelling to meet people), Richard Feynman (the pleasure of finding things out, bongos, safecracking).

Richard Feynman never figured out the meaning of life. So he played instead.

At Los Alamos, during the war, he taught himself to open the safes holding atomic secrets. Just to see if he could.

He drummed bongos. He took up drawing in his forties. He puzzled over ancient Maya astronomy, for fun.

One day in a Cornell cafeteria, someone tossed a plate into the air.

He watched it wobble and spin, and worked out the motion. No purpose. Just play. As he told it, that game led him back to the work that won a Nobel Prize.

He called all of it the same thing: the pleasure of finding things out.

He never needed the final answer. He said he could live with doubt, and uncertainty, and not knowing.

So pick one thing today. A lock, a clock, a recipe. Take it apart. Just to see how it works.
