# Fly one camera, not twelve slides

The best-documented "step inside a famous painting" loops, Mike Staniforth's included, are **one continuous 14–20 second Seedance 2.5 image-to-video generation**, not a stitched montage. Staniforth starts each one from an **AI-outpainted 9:16 first frame at exactly 1080×1920**. He does not crop the painting. His prompt describes a camera route and gives each stretch of it a time budget. Macro details happen *while the camera travels*, not as separate stops. The route ends by pushing into a reflective surface (the lieutenant's eye in Night Watch, a coffee cup in Nighthawks) that contains the opening image, and a light edit cleans up the join. The one complete public prompt in this exact format, from Alavisuals, follows the same rules. Subjects stay frozen and the camera does all the moving. Things get bigger because the camera physically approaches them, not because of an optical zoom. Cuts, stops and dissolves are banned outright. The slideshow look you got from Seedance on Higgsfield fits three things the sources warn about. A cropped first frame gives the model less depth to fly through. A prompt in labelled blocks with many beats reads as separate shots, and Seedance tends to cut between shots by default. And if the model was Seedance 2.0, its 15-second cap makes a 20-second single take impossible. Evidence is thin in several places. No Reddit content was reachable. X and Instagram were seen only through search snippets. Staniforth's actual Night Watch prompt sits in an email-gated kit that was not downloaded.

## Staniforth's loops are one 20-second Seedance 2.5 take

Staniforth's own site is the best primary source, and it is unusually open about method. The Night Watch kit page sells the format in one line: one painting, twenty seconds, a camera move that comes full circle. The free kit contains three things. The first is a PNG first frame you upload to anchor composition, light and characters. The second is a prompt for "the continuous camera route, moving macro details and lieutenant's eye-reflection reveal". The third is a loop guide with camera timings and a checklist for matching the final frame to the first ([mikestaniforth.com/night-watch](https://mikestaniforth.com/night-watch)). He also says the first frame is an **AI-assisted vertical adaptation** of Rembrandt's 1642 painting, and that the macro journey and eye reflection are imagined extensions of it ([mikestaniforth.com/night-watch](https://mikestaniforth.com/night-watch)). The Nighthawks kit, for the piece he says passed **2 million Instagram views**, is built the same way. It includes a 1080×1920 first frame and a higher-resolution reference, a prompt covering the camera route, the coffee-cup macro and the painting-in-the-coffee reveal, and notes on 20-second timings and speed ramps ([mikestaniforth.com/nighthawks](https://mikestaniforth.com/nighthawks)).

His Great Wave making-of is the most detailed process account of this format anywhere. He works on a Morphic canvas, keeping the original, the outpaints and the video attempts side by side. He brings the original print in and uses **Expand Artboard to extend it to 9:16**, describing the continuation in an outpainting prompt and checking it against the source. He then runs **Seedance 2.5 in Morphic** with the outpaint as first-frame reference and a 20-second image-to-video prompt ([mikestaniforth.com/great-wave](https://mikestaniforth.com/great-wave)). Two details matter for you. First, his outpaint came out at 1088×1920, and Morphic's Copilot warned him that **Seedance inherits the source image's aspect ratio**, so he cropped four pixels off each side to reach 1080×1920 ([mikestaniforth.com/great-wave](https://mikestaniforth.com/great-wave)). Second, he describes the prompt as giving each part of the journey a time budget, with macro moments happening while the camera keeps moving, and a final view that matches the first. The finish happens in Morphic's Compose editor: trim the loop, check the join, add sound, export, then watch whether the move flows into another lap ([mikestaniforth.com/great-wave](https://mikestaniforth.com/great-wave)).

The single-take reading is partly inference, but a strong one. Seedance 2.5 supports single takes **up to 30 seconds** and first-frame or first-plus-last-frame input ([Morphic guide](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/seedance-2-5-guide); [ByteDance Seed](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/one-take-creation-flexible-referencing-introducing-seedance-2-5)). Seedance 2.0 stops at 15 seconds ([dev.to](https://dev.to/owen_fox/seedance-25-image-to-video-first-and-last-frame-control-2026-2684)). Staniforth's edit steps are trim, speed ramp and sound, with no mention of joining clips. He never mentions a last-frame anchor, so the return to the opening frame seems to come from the prompt plus the trim. Whether he also feeds the same image as the last frame is **unconfirmed**. One variant does use more inputs: his Pantheon piece used **five 9:16 reference frames** (exterior, fountain, doorway, painting on the wall, painting extended to 9:16) to plan a readable route through a journey that changes location ([mikestaniforth.com/pantheon](https://mikestaniforth.com/pantheon)). Even there the references go into the generation itself, not into an edit. Around Staniforth, a small set of copycats and templates followed his September 2026 Nighthawks success: Alavisuals (1 October), a prompts.chat template (1 November), an Artlist "Inside the Canvas" preset and a TikTok creator using the same comment-for-prompt hook ([alavisuals.com](https://alavisuals.com/blog/turn-a-painting-into-a-cinematic-fly-through-with-seedance-2-5/); [prompts.chat](https://prompts.chat/prompts/cmvxp30cs0005jw081tltygox_step-inside-a-painting-frozen-3d-camera-fly-through-image-to-video); [artlist.io](https://artlist.io/ai/presets)). The Artlist and TikTok items are known only from search snippets.

The prompt-length question has a resolution. One 2.0 expert caps prompts at 60–100 words and reports that a prompt with eight requirements typically gets four or five honoured ([heyuan110](https://www.heyuan110.com/posts/ai/2026-07-11-seedance-2-prompt-guide/)). Yet the working 2.5 painting prompts run to several hundred words. Length is not what separates them. What the words describe is. The Alavisuals prompt is long, but almost every sentence constrains *one* camera path: its speed, its route between neighbouring subjects, its lens behaviour, what it must not do. A long prompt that lists many scenes asks for many shots. A long prompt that describes one trajectory asks for one shot.

## Frozen subjects, physical camera travel and a reflection hinge create the depth

Every working source converges on the same rules. The Alavisuals prompt (Seedance 2.5, 20 s, silent, painting as `@Image 1` exact first frame) is the fullest public template. Paraphrased, it treats the painting as a 3D reconstruction that keeps its framing, identities, poses, style, lighting and colours. It freezes every subject so the camera creates all the motion, allowing at most a blink, a hair shift or fabric reacting. The camera flies like a tiny cinema drone along one connected route through the gaps between neighbouring subjects, mixing height changes with forward travel in diagonal sweeps. The lens stays constant, so things grow through **physical approach, not optical zoom**, while foreground surfaces cross the lens in parallax. Hovering, stops, repeated push-ins, cuts and dissolves are all banned. Time ranges of roughly 0–4, 4–10, 10–16, 16–18 and 18–20 s are labelled as checkpoints along one flight. At the end the camera pushes into an eye (or glass, metal or water if there is no eye), reveals the full opening image in the reflection, and keeps advancing until that image resolves into the exact first frame, slowing to match the opening speed. The full text is at [alavisuals.com](https://alavisuals.com/blog/turn-a-painting-into-a-cinematic-fly-through-with-seedance-2-5/). The prompts.chat "frozen 3D camera fly-through" template takes the same idea further, forbidding any painted element from moving while the camera pushes, orbits and drifts for parallax ([prompts.chat](https://prompts.chat/prompts/cmvxp30cs0005jw081tltygox_step-inside-a-painting-frozen-3d-camera-fly-through-image-to-video)). A HitPaw Last Supper prompt for Seedance 2.5, visible only as a snippet because X returned an error, uses the painting as both **exact first frame and matching final frame** in one continuous camera move ([HitPaw on X](https://x.com/HitPawofficial/status/2110213751362531528)).

The freeze rule matters for identity drift as well as depth. The reports of drift (extra people, faces reshaping, costume changes) come mostly from Kling, from users animating people over longer clips, and through secondhand summaries ([virse.ai](https://www.virse.ai/blog/kling-4-0-reddit-reviews); [diyai.io](https://diyai.io/ai-tools/video-generation/reviews/kling-ai-review/)). No source explains why freezing helps. The plausible reading is that a frozen face gives the model less to regenerate. Kling's own painting guide makes the same point from the other side: big gestures, smiles and head turns break the illusion, and busy compositions without one clear focal point produce artifacts ([Kling](https://kling.ai/topics/animate-painting-ai)). The Night Watch is exactly that kind of busy composition. That is a reason to give the camera a clear route between named figures rather than asking the model to animate the crowd.

Two optional levers can add depth. Seedance accepts **reference videos for camera motion**: up to 3 clips totalling 15 s on 2.0, and 10 clips totalling 30 s on 2.5. The pattern is "follow the camera movement of @Video1, do not copy its subject" ([fal](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.0/reference-to-video/api); [Runway](https://runway.com/resources/seedance-2-5-prompt-guide); [heyuan110](https://www.heyuan110.com/posts/ai/2026-07-11-seedance-2-prompt-guide/)). On Higgsfield this is the @motion role tag ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/generating-with-seedance-2-0)). No painting creator documents using one, and on 2.5 the API rejects jobs that combine frame images with reference inputs ([dev.to](https://dev.to/owen_fox/seedance-25-image-to-video-first-and-last-frame-control-2026-2684)), so it trades off against exact endpoints. The second lever is a **drawn path**. Mervin Praison paints a pink route on the still and tells Seedance to treat it as camera guidance and remove it. He reports that the lines sometimes leak into the video and that rooftops duplicate ([mer.vin](https://mer.vin/2026/06/draw-camera-paths-on-images-flora-seedance-fpv-drone-motion-control/)).

On "photoreal faces and fibre-level detail" the sources pull in different directions. Alavisuals and HitPaw both insist the painting keep its material character and stay recognisably the painter's work. Your target clip shows photoreal skin and thread. Nobody documents a photoreal pre-pass or A/B test on paintings. Staniforth's "AI-assisted vertical adaptation" first frame may well be partly re-rendered, but he doesn't say which image model he used or how far it departs from the original. The guidance on macro detail is generic: name the surface and its micro-structure (weave, individual fibres, brushed metal), use raking light and shallow depth of field, and validate motion at 720p before rendering at 1080p ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/how-to-prompt-seedance-2-5-a-guide-for-ai-video-creators/); [Morphic 4K guide](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/seedance-2-4k-guide), snippet).

## Kling, Veo and Seedance 2.0 push you into multi-clip chaining

If Seedance 2.5 is not available to you, the multi-clip route is well documented, just not for this exact format. The standard chain works like this. Make keyframe stills in Nano Banana or GPT Image 2. Animate each consecutive pair with start/end frames. Extract the last frame of clip N and use it as the first frame of clip N+1. Join in an editor. Tao Prompts teaches this on Higgsfield with GPT Image 2 and Seedance 2.0, splitting work into 15-second rows with `[00:00-00:04]` time ranges ([Modern Creator Network](https://modcreator-app.vercel.app/2026-05-01-tao-prompts-create-seamless-ai-films-of-any-length-gpt-image-2-seedance-2-0)). Curious Refuge and Masonry teach it with Nano Banana and Kling, with the warning that **if the model doesn't reach the end frame, the gap is too big**: split it in two ([Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-use-nano-banana-for-filmmaking); [Masonry](https://masonry.so/blog/exploded-view-animation-guide-nanobananapro-kling)). The weak point is the seam. Kling's end frame is a target the model only tries to resemble. Transitions tear when start and end frames differ in lens feel, height, light or scale ([Atlas Cloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/kling-4-controlled-camera-movement), snippet). Users report 10-second Kling clips drifting in their second half ([virse.ai](https://www.virse.ai/blog/kling-4-0-reddit-reviews)).

The closest single-tool alternative on Higgsfield is **Kling 4.0 Frames mode**. A reviewer testing on Higgsfield found it accepts a start/end pair or up to 10 keyframes over a 3–30 s slider, and hit all four keyframes in order in a test ([Local Lab Digest](https://www.locallabdigest.com/blog/kling-4-0-tutorial)). Nobody has shown it on a painting journey. It also brings back the keyframe-design burden, and the same reviewer notes that small details slip. Veo appears in the sources only as preserving artwork style in image-to-video ([Replicate](https://replicate.com/blog/veo-3-image), snippet), with a warning that it tends to add realistic texture and extra movement ([elser.ai](https://www.elser.ai/blog/kling-vs-seedance-vs-veo-for-anime-videos-which-ai-model-wins-in-2026)). No creator documents a Veo painting loop. On endpoint accuracy there is one measurement. In single 5-second runs, Seedance 2.5 landed within a mean pixel difference of **2.0 at 720p** of the requested last frame. Seedance 2.0 Mini scored 35.9, close to the 39.0 between two unrelated images, so it effectively ignored the last frame ([dev.to](https://dev.to/owen_fox/seedance-25-image-to-video-first-and-last-frame-control-2026-2684)).

## A recommended workflow for a 20-second 9:16 Night Watch loop

The recipe below copies the sourced Staniforth/Alavisuals pattern wherever it can. It marks where it relies on inference.

| Step | Action | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Model | Use **Seedance 2.5** for a single 20 s take. Check that your Higgsfield account offers 2.5; the Higgsfield toolkit in this workspace lists Seedance 2.0. If it doesn't, run 2.5 on Morphic (Staniforth's host), Runway, Krea, fal or Together. If you must stay on 2.0, see the fallback below | 2.5 allows 4–30 s, 2.0 stops at 15 s ([dev.to](https://dev.to/owen_fox/seedance-25-image-to-video-first-and-last-frame-control-2026-2684)); Higgsfield 2.5 availability unconfirmed in sources |
| 2. First frame | Don't crop. Take a high-res scan, keep the central group (captain, lieutenant, girl in gold) at readable scale, and **outpaint top and bottom** to 9:16 with an image model. Export at exactly **1080×1920**. Optionally regenerate faces and fabrics at higher detail while keeping identities and poses | Staniforth's Expand Artboard method and pixel fix ([great-wave](https://mikestaniforth.com/great-wave)); the detail pass is inference |
| 3. Route | Plan 3–4 waypoints on the frame: foreground gap, a macro surface, a mid-depth pass, then the lieutenant's eye. One continuous path, no location changes | Staniforth's lieutenant eye-reflection ending ([night-watch](https://mikestaniforth.com/night-watch)); 6–10 s beat guidance ([Runway](https://runway.com/resources/seedance-2-5-prompt-guide)) |
| 4. Prompt | Camera-only language, frozen subjects, time ranges marked as checkpoints along one flight, an explicit "no cuts", and an eased return to frame 1 (draft below). Never use the word "zoom". Don't call it a night scene: the darkness comes from aged varnish | Alavisuals pattern; Higgsfield no-cuts tip ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/seedance-prompting-guide)); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Watch) |
| 5. Endpoints | A/B test: (a) first frame only, with the loop described in prose (likely Staniforth); (b) the same image as first and last frame in Keyframe mode with no other references (HitPaw, Krea) | [Krea](https://www.krea.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-prompt-guide); [Runway](https://runway.com/resources/seedance-2-5-prompt-guide); [dev.to](https://dev.to/owen_fox/seedance-25-image-to-video-first-and-last-frame-control-2026-2684) |
| 6. Drafts | 3–4 drafts at 720p, change one variable per take, re-render the winner at 1080p with audio off | [Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/generating-with-seedance-2-0); [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/create/seedance-2-0-mini-camera-control-tips); [seedance.tv](https://www.seedance.tv/blog/seedance-miricanvas-image-to-video-workflow-2026) |
| 7. Finish | Trim to the cleanest join and add speed ramps. If the seam shows, add a short crossfade of a few frames. Add sound design. Play it on repeat | Staniforth's finishing steps ([great-wave](https://mikestaniforth.com/great-wave)); crossfade fix ([Andy Cinquin](https://andy-cinquin.com/blog/midjourney-video-to-seamless-loop-ffmpeg)); frame count is inference |

Here is an original starting prompt. It applies the sourced rules and has not been tested. Name the figures by what is visible, because the model sees the image, not the art history.

```
Use @Image1 as the exact first frame. One uninterrupted 20-second shot:
a tiny, expertly piloted cinema drone flies into this militia company as a
real three-dimensional space and returns to the opening view through a
reflection. Every figure holds its painted pose; only the camera moves.
Allowed life: a blink, a breath, a stirring feather, dust drifting in light.

The lens never changes. Things grow larger only because the camera travels
toward them. Continuous curving momentum: while passing one subject it is
already steering to the next. Lance shafts, sleeves and banners slide past
the lens in the foreground, revealing depth behind. No cuts, dissolves,
pauses or repeated push-ins.

0-6s: hold the full view for half a second, then glide low between the
captain in black and the lieutenant in pale gold. Skim his embroidered coat
at macro distance, gold thread and individual fibres sharp, without stopping.
6-13s: rise in a diagonal arc past the musketeers and the girl lit in gold,
close enough to read skin pores, beard hairs and the sheen of a steel helmet,
then curve back down through a gap between the pikes.
13-17s: sweep toward the lieutenant's face and approach his eye steadily:
iris fibres, wet highlight, lashes.
17-20s: the complete opening composition appears in the curved reflection of
his eye. Keep advancing until it fills the frame and flattens into the exact
first frame, same scale, positions, light and colour, at the same gentle
speed as the opening.

Keep the original light direction and deep shadows of a daytime scene.
Preserve every face, costume and pose; stable anatomy, no new people.
Silent video, no music, no subtitles, no text.
```

If the result still looks flat, cut the macro checkpoints first, then the number of figures visited. Fewer waypoints with more physical travel between them beat more beats. If faces drift, route the camera beside faces rather than straight into them, except for the final eye. The fallback for Seedance 2.0 on Higgsfield is two generations, 15 s plus 5 s. Clip 2 starts from clip 1's extracted last frame and ends on the original first frame as its end keyframe ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/how-to-prompt-seedance-2-5-a-guide-for-ai-video-creators/); [Tao Prompts](https://modcreator-app.vercel.app/2026-05-01-tao-prompts-create-seamless-ai-films-of-any-length-gpt-image-2-seedance-2-0)). Put the join mid-flight, not at a waypoint. Budget per attempt: at API pricing of **$0.48/s at 1080p**, a 20 s take costs about $9.60, against about $4.80 at 720p ([dev.to](https://dev.to/owen_fox/seedance-25-image-to-video-first-and-last-frame-control-2026-2684)). Higgsfield credits will differ.

## Conclusion

This format is less a prompt trick than a change of subject: the shot is about the camera, and the painting is just the set. Staniforth's real advantage seems to be preparation more than wording. He outpaints a vertical set with room to fly through, sizes it to the exact pixel, and writes one route the model can't break into shots. That explains why a cropped first frame plus a many-beat content prompt gives a slideshow even on the same model. Expect the main remaining problem to be the seam, not depth. Testing whether a last-frame anchor beats a prose-described return is the cheapest unanswered experiment, and well worth running.

For your channels, the format also scales in a useful way. Staniforth has published more than twenty paintings in about two months on one template, each with a different reflective "hinge" (eye, coffee, coin) chosen from what the painting already contains. Building your own library of route-plus-hinge templates per painting, plus a standard outpainting step, should matter more than refining any single prompt.
