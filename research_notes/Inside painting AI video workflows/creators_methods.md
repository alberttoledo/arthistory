# Creators and methods behind "inside the painting" AI loop videos

Research date: 4 Nov 2026. Primary source is Mike Staniforth's own website. I read its static HTML and the publicly served JS bundles, which hold the page copy and SEO metadata. X, Instagram and TikTok posts could not be fetched directly, so anything from those platforms comes from search-engine snippets and is marked as such. The kits are email-gated and I did not submit an email, so the actual prompt text in his kits is NOT included here.

## 1. What has Mike Staniforth publicly said/shared about his workflow, tools and prompts?

### Takeaway
Staniforth openly describes his painting loops as **single image-to-video generations in ByteDance Seedance 2.5**. Each one starts from an **AI-made (outpainted/adapted) 9:16 first-frame still**, follows a time-budgeted 14–20 s camera-route prompt and has a "loop guide" for matching the last frame to the first. He finishes in an editor: trim, check the join, speed ramps, sound. Since about Oct 2026 he has worked inside **Morphic**: Canvas, then Expand Artboard outpainting, then Copilot, then Seedance 2.5, then the Compose editor. Every painting has a free email-gated kit with a PNG, the prompt and a guide; the prompts are not public otherwise.

- Rousseau tiger video prompt (verbatim): "Use the supplied layers and assembled composition. Build one paper jungle from back to front. ### Inferences
- The "Comment NIGHTWATCH" CTA most likely funnels to the email-gated /night-watch kit, which feeds his MailerLite list, which upsells £125 sessions, £350 commissions and the coming course. (Inference from site structure.)
- Seedance 2.5 does single takes up to 30 s ([Morphic guide](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/seedance-2-5-guide); [ByteDance Seed blog](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/one-take-creation-flexible-referencing-introducing-seedance-2-5)). Together with his "one painting, twenty seconds, a camera move that comes full circle" wording, this strongly suggests each painting loop is **one generation**, not stitched clips. Edit-stage work is a trim, speed ramps and sound. His earliest loops (Nighthawks, Caravaggio, Sep 2026) are described only as "Seedance". The exact version is not confirmed for those, but 20 s exceeds Seedance 2.0's 15 s cap, which points to 2.5.
- Why "first frame only" loops still close: the prompt explicitly describes the return to the opening composition, and the loop guide plus editor trim handle the seam. He does not mention using a last-frame anchor. Possibly first+last frame (same image) is used; this is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- The exact Night Watch, Nighthawks and Caravaggio prompt text is only in the email-gated ZIPs, which I did not download.
- I could not read his Instagram/X captions directly. I can't confirm whether early pieces used Higgsfield, Kling or other hosts before Morphic.
- He doesn't say which image model made the "AI-assisted vertical adaptation" first frames for Night Watch/Nighthawks. For Great Wave it was Morphic's Expand Artboard; its underlying model is not named.

## 3. One generation or multiple clips? Breakdowns of the edit

### Takeaway
Primary-source evidence points to **one continuous Seedance 2.5 image-to-video take (14–20 s)** per loop. The edit is light: trim to the seam, speed ramps, sound design, and checking the end-to-start join. Multi-reference stills are used for more complex journeys (Pantheon), but those are still passed as references in a generation.

### Cited Findings
- Staniforth: "One painting. Twenty seconds. A camera move that comes full circle." / "20 seconds / continuous loop" — [mikestaniforth.com/night-watch](https://mikestaniforth.com/night-watch)
- His edit step is described as "trim the loop, check the join, add sound and export" in Morphic Compose — [mikestaniforth.com/great-wave](https://mikestaniforth.com/great-wave)
- His guides include "speed-ramp notes" and "a finishing checklist for bringing the final frame back to the first" — [mikestaniforth.com/nighthawks](https://mikestaniforth.com/nighthawks)
- Alavisuals' prompt demands "one connected route" with "No hovering, stops, repeated push-ins, abrupt steering, cuts or dissolves" in one 20 s generation — [alavisuals.com](https://alavisuals.com/blog/turn-a-painting-into-a-cinematic-fly-through-with-seedance-2-5/)
- Seedance 2.5 capabilities: single takes up to 30 s; first frame or first+last frame; up to 50 references; Edit (region) and Extend (before/after/bridge two clips). "Name each anchor frame separately in the prompt" — [Morphic guide](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/seedance-2-5-guide); [ByteDance Seed](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/one-take-creation-flexible-referencing-introducing-seedance-2-5)
- First/last-frame aspect ratios must match or the last frame may stretch — [clipdance.ai](https://clipdance.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-first-last-frame-prompt-workflow); [runcomfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/first-last-frame)

### Inferences
- An alternative for imperfect seams: Seedance 2.5 Extend can "bridge" the final frames back into the opening clip. No creator documented doing this for these loops, so this is speculation.

### Gaps
- I found no frame-by-frame or timeline screenshot breakdown of the Night Watch edit itself. The Great Wave page has a Morphic canvas screenshot, which I could not view as an image.

## 4. Publicly shared full prompts

### Takeaway
The only full, public, painting-to-eye-reflection loop prompt I found is Alavisuals' Seedance 2.5 prompt, quoted below in full. Staniforth's painting prompts are gated; his public Layers prompts are quoted in section 1.

### Cited Findings
- **Alavisuals, Seedance 2.5, image reference = painting (1 Oct 2026)**, verbatim — [alavisuals.com](https://alavisuals.com/blog/turn-a-painting-into-a-cinematic-fly-through-with-seedance-2-5/):

> Use @Image 1 as the exact first frame and visual reference. Create a 20-second uninterrupted cinematic micro-FPV flight into a three-dimensional reconstruction of the painting, travelling through its principal subjects before entering a reflection of the complete image and returning seamlessly to the opening composition. Preserve the painting's framing, identities, poses, objects, visual style, lighting, colours and negative space. All subjects remain frozen; the camera creates the movement.
>
> Build one connected route around the painting's actual composition, following neighbouring subjects through accessible gaps across foreground, middle ground and background. Visit every principal character individually, including those towards the outer edges. For paintings without people, explore the main animals, objects, architectural features or landscape areas instead. Distribute attention across the complete composition, giving each subject a brief readable moving passage. Details and textures appear naturally during these passes without becoming prolonged inspections.
>
> The camera moves like a tiny, expertly piloted cinema drone, carrying continuous forward momentum through smooth curves. As it passes one subject, it is already steering towards the next. Combine changes in height with forward and lateral travel, creating gentle diagonal sweeps rather than separate upward or downward movements. Maintain a consistent lens perspective: subjects grow through physical approach, not optical zoom. Nearby surfaces cross the foreground through parallax, revealing the next destination beyond. No hovering, stops, repeated push-ins, abrupt steering, cuts or dissolves.
>
> During 0–4 seconds, begin moving inward immediately, keeping the complete opening readable for approximately half a second before accelerating towards an accessible foreground subject near one side. Pass beside it and continue directly towards its neighbour. During 4–10 seconds, weave through successive subjects and deeper layers, keeping each face, gesture or distinctive form briefly readable while travelling. During 10–16 seconds, cover the remaining subjects towards the opposite side and curve naturally towards the final reflection destination. These intervals mark progress along one flight, not separate shots.
>
> Preserve solid anatomy, stable geometry, object dimensions and contact points throughout. Travel around bodies and surfaces through open space. Retain the painting's original material character and stylisation as its world becomes dimensional. Reveal existing skin, hair, fabric, feathers, foliage, wood, stone, metal or glass through proximity. Keep illumination anchored to the original scene, with reflections changing naturally as the viewpoint moves. Use continuous focus transfers, enough depth to understand neighbouring subjects, and restrained directional motion blur during faster passages.
>
> During 16–18 seconds, continue directly into an extreme close-up of an accessible eye belonging to the final subject. If no eye is available, use an existing reflective surface such as glass, metal or water; if neither exists, use a distinctive existing surface detail. Progressively reveal the complete opening image within its reflection or texture, preserving the exact orientation, subjects, framing and colours. This recursive image is the only deliberate surreal addition, with no glowing portal or decorative effects.
>
> During 18–20 seconds, keep advancing into the miniature image as it expands to fill the frame. Its curvature or texture gradually resolves into the undistorted opening perspective while the surrounding surface passes beyond view. Finish on the exact first-frame composition, matching scale, positions, lighting and colour. Ease the apparent forward speed to match the gentle opening movement so the repeat flows directly into the next flight. Silent video, no dialogue, music, subtitles or added text.

- prompts.chat "frozen 3D fly-through" (Grok-tagged, 1 Nov 2026). The full text is on the page; the summarizer limited quoting. Key line: "No person, animal, object, water, foliage, cloud, flame, or any other painted element moves" — [prompts.chat](https://prompts.chat/prompts/cmvxp30cs0005jw081tltygox_step-inside-a-painting-frozen-3d-camera-fly-through-image-to-video)

### Inferences
- The common recipe across sources:
  1. Use a 9:16 AI-outpainted or adapted still of the painting as the first frame.
  2. Write a timecoded single-route camera prompt (micro-FPV/drone, physical approach rather than zoom, "frozen" subjects with minimal micro-motion).
  3. Name the macro beats: coin, coffee cup, eye.
  4. End by pushing into a reflective surface (eye, coffee, wine, glass) that contains the opening image, then resolve to the exact first frame with eased speed.
  5. Trim and add sound in an editor.

### Gaps
- I found no public wine-glass-reflection variant prompt. Staniforth's analogues are the coffee cup (Nighthawks) and coin (Caravaggio).
- I found no Higgsfield blog or community prompt specific to painting eye-reflection loops.
